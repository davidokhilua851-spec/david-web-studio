Services

Four ways to move freight, one desk to book them.

Every mode below runs through the same dispatch team and the same tracking system — you don't juggle separate vendors for LTL versus truckload.

01 · FTL

Full truckload

A dedicated trailer for your freight only — no shared space, no intermediate stops, direct from dock to dock.

Best for shipments of 10+ pallets, time-sensitive freight, or anything that shouldn't be handled by a third party mid-route. We dispatch dry van, temperature-controlled reefer, and flatbed equipment depending on what you're moving.

Typical load size
10–26 pallets
Equipment
Dry van, reefer, flatbed
Avg. transit
450–500 mi/day
Rate basis
Per mile + fuel surcharge
02 · LTL

Less-than-truckload

Ship a partial load without paying for a trailer you don't need.

Your pallets share trailer space with compatible freight heading the same direction. Slightly longer transit than FTL since freight is consolidated and cross-docked, but the cost per pallet is a fraction of a dedicated truck.

Typical load size
1–6 pallets
Equipment
Shared dry van
Avg. transit
2–5 business days
Rate basis
Weight class + distance
03 · IM

Intermodal

Rail for the long haul, truck for the first and last mile.

On lanes over 700 miles, moving the container by rail for the middle leg is usually cheaper per mile than truck the whole way, with a modest tradeoff in transit time. We handle drayage on both ends so it still looks like one shipment to you.

Best lane length
700+ miles
Equipment
53' container, truck drayage
Avg. transit
1–2 days longer than FTL
Rate basis
Container + drayage fees
04 · WH

Warehousing

Short-term storage and cross-docking so freight doesn't idle on a trailer.

14 regional hubs handle overflow inventory, staging for multi-stop deliveries, and cross-docking between inbound and outbound loads — useful when a delivery window doesn't line up with your pickup.

Locations
14 regional hubs
Storage terms
Short-term, by the pallet-day
Services
Cross-dock, staging, relabeling
Rate basis
Pallet-day + handling
FAQ

Common questions

How fast can I get a rate?

Most lanes return a binding rate within 11 minutes through the dispatch desk. You can also run a self-serve estimate instantly with the rate calculator, which uses the same pricing logic.

Do you carry cargo insurance?

Every carrier in our network carries a minimum of $100,000 in cargo insurance, verified before they're eligible for dispatch. Higher-value loads can be covered with supplemental coverage on request.

Can I track a shipment without an account?

Yes — the tracker works with just a shipment ID, no login required. Creating an account lets you save quotes and see shipment history in one place.

What happens if a load is delayed?

Your dispatch contact is notified automatically and reaches out with a revised ETA before you have to ask. Weather and mechanical delays are the two most common causes, both are visible in shipment tracking in real time.

About

We got tired of freight brokers who disappear after booking.

Meridian started as a three-person dispatch desk in Atlanta and grew by keeping the same promise: someone answers the phone, and the ETA on the screen is the truth.

Timeline

How we got here

2016

Founded in Atlanta

Started with a single lane — Atlanta to Chicago — and a spreadsheet. Three people, four trucks, one promise: pick up the phone every time.

2019

Carrier network passes 1,000

Built out a vetting process for carriers — safety scores, insurance verification, on-time history — before opening the network beyond our first regional partners.

2021

Launched live tracking

Replaced check-in phone calls with GPS-based tracking, cutting the average "where's my truck" call by more than half.

2023

Added intermodal & warehousing

Opened the first regional cross-dock hub and brought rail intermodal in-house instead of subcontracting it out.

2026

6,400+ carriers, 48 states

Today the network covers the continental U.S. with a 99.2% on-time rate — the number we watch more closely than any other.
